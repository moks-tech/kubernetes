Reaching /postgres and /redis from your laptop only

mk4 (jithendar.com) · what to do next · written 20 November 2026 · runbook: docs/cloudflare-access-and-db-backups.md

In short. https://jithendar.com/postgres/ (pgAdmin) and /redis/ (Redis Insight) answer RBAC: access denied because jithendar.com now goes through Cloudflare, and their IP allowlist only ever sees Cloudflare's addresses. Today, open them through kubectl port-forward (section 2): only a machine with your kubeconfig can do that. For good, put Cloudflare Access in front, signed in as you and limited to your Mac by its serial number (section 3), then merge the step-8 change (section 4).

2. Today: open them from your laptop, nothing to change

kubectl port-forward goes through the Kubernetes API with your kubeconfig, which only your laptop holds. The gateway, Cloudflare and both allowlists are not involved, and nothing is exposed to anyone else.

pgAdmin

kubectl -n postgres-db port-forward svc/postgres-ui 8080:80
# then open http://localhost:8080/postgres/  (pgAdmin's own login, then a database login)

Redis Insight

kubectl -n redis port-forward svc/redis-ui 8081:80
# then open http://localhost:8081/redis/  (signs in to Valkey as the read-mostly "console" user)

Command-line clients (home network only, unchanged)

psql "host=postgres.jithendar.com port=443 dbname=<name> user=<name> sslmode=verify-full sslrootcert=system sslnegotiation=direct"
redis-cli --tls --sni redis.jithendar.com -h redis.jithendar.com -p 443 --user <name> --askpass

Both stop working when your ISP changes your home address: update postgres_allowed_ips and redis_allowed_ips in minikube-infrastructure/vars/mk4.tfvars. Your current IPv4 address is the ip= line of curl -4 -s https://jithendar.com/cdn-cgi/trace.

3. For good: Cloudflare Access, you and this Mac only

Cloudflare asks for your login before a request reaches mk4. The gateway then refuses those paths unless the request carries Cloudflare's signed Access token, so going around Cloudflare to the server's address gets 403. Two Access applications are used, so the databases can have a stricter rule than the other admin tools:

Access applicationPaths on jithendar.comPolicy
jithendar.com databases/postgres, /redisInclude Emails = your address
Require Device Posture – Serial Number List = your Mac
jithendar.com admin tools/argocd, /kiali, /grafana, /prometheus, /loki, /ui, /v1 (Vault)Include Emails = your address. A second policy, Service Auth, for the moks-ci service token.
two Bypass applications/prometheus/api/v1/write, /loki/api/v1/pushBypass, Include Everyone. The EKS Prometheus agent and Alloy push here with their own gateway login.

In an Access policy, Include rules work like OR and Require rules like AND: with the rule above you must be signed in as you and be on your Mac. Without the second application, the serial-number rule would also apply to Argo CD, Grafana and the rest.

Steps in Cloudflare (dash.cloudflare.com and one.dash.cloudflare.com)

  1. SSL/TLS → Overview: encryption mode Full (strict). The origin has a valid Let's Encrypt certificate.
  2. Zero Trust: pick a team name. Your team domain is <team>.cloudflareaccess.com. Under Settings → Authentication add One-time PIN (or Google).
  3. Enrol your Mac: install the Cloudflare One (WARP) client, allow your email under the device enrolment settings, and sign the client in to your team. Posture only mode is enough: it reports device information without routing your traffic.
  4. Serial number list: on the Mac run system_profiler SPHardwareDataType | grep 'Serial Number'. In Zero Trust → Reusable components → Lists, create a list of type Serial numbers with that value.
  5. Applications: Access → Applications → Add → Self-hosted, as in the table above. Copy each of the two main applications' Application Audience (AUD) tag (Overview tab).
  6. Service token (optional for now): Access → Service credentials → create moks-ci, and store its ID and secret as the CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_ID and CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_SECRET Actions secrets of the moks-sre repository. Only its Argo CD deploy job uses them, and that job is off today.
Check before relying on it. Cloudflare's docs list device serial numbers as supported on macOS, Windows and Linux, but do not say which Zero Trust plans include them. If your plan does not, use Require IP ranges instead: your home IPv4 address and your IPv6 /64 prefix (both from https://jithendar.com/cdn-cgi/trace). That means "your home network" rather than "your laptop", and it needs updating when the ISP changes either address. Access client certificates (mTLS) are another way to pin one device, but they need a pay-as-you-go or Enterprise Zero Trust plan.

4. Turn it on in mk4 (runbook step 8)

Send the team domain and the two AUD tags (none of them is secret). The change to minikube-infrastructure/vars/mk4.tfvars is:

cloudflare_access = {
  team_domain = "<team>.cloudflareaccess.com"
  audiences   = ["<AUD of jithendar.com databases>", "<AUD of jithendar.com admin tools>"]
}

Merging it to master applies it (workflow Create minikube infrastructure). Terraform then makes the gateway require an Access token on all nine paths and switches off the /postgres and /redis IP allowlists. postgres.jithendar.com and redis.jithendar.com keep theirs. Do steps 2–5 of section 3 first: once this is applied, any of the nine paths without an Access application in front answers 403.

Check it

# Through Cloudflare, not signed in: a redirect to your Access login
curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code} %{redirect_url}\n' https://jithendar.com/postgres/
# Around Cloudflare, straight to the server: refused by the gateway
curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' --resolve jithendar.com:443:46.224.66.128 https://jithendar.com/postgres/   # 403
# The site itself is unaffected
curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' https://jithendar.com/                                                       # 200

In a browser on your Mac, /postgres/ asks for the Access login, then pgAdmin's. On another device, signed in as you, Cloudflare refuses it. Grafana's dashboards for the EKS cluster should keep receiving data (the two Bypass paths).

One detail: the gateway accepts a token from either Access application on all nine paths. The Mac-only rule is applied by Cloudflare. Someone who went around Cloudflare would therefore need a valid admin-tools token, which is only issued after signing in as you.

6. If something goes wrong

You seeMeaning, and what to do
Every page: RBAC: access deniedA gateway policy refuses Cloudflare itself. That happened on 20 Nov (fixed in #20). Remove the newest policy in istio-ingress, or revert the last change to mk4.tfvars.
/postgres or /redis: RBAC: access deniedStep 8 is not applied yet (allowlist), or the request reached mk4 without an Access token. Use section 2 meanwhile.
401 Jwt verification failsThe token's audience is not in audiences, or the team domain is wrong. Compare with the AUD tags in Zero Trust.
Cloudflare's "you don't have access" pageThe policy refused you: wrong email, or the Mac's client is not signed in or its serial number is not on the list.
EKS dashboards stop receiving dataThe two Bypass applications are missing or have the wrong path.

Roll back step 8: remove cloudflare_access from mk4.tfvars and merge. The allowlists come back, and /postgres and /redis are 403 again behind Cloudflare. Then delete the applications in Zero Trust.
